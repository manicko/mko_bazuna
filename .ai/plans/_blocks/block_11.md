## 3. Priority

**Medium.** Block 11 spans three layers of differing risk:

- **V1, V2 (LOW)** — Modal visibility and pre-fill are static/template-context assertions with no execution risk. They are regression guardrails for the FT-002 feature surface.
- **V3, V4 (LOW)** — The create handler and cabinet management are fully or mostly covered; remaining gaps are narrow edge cases (cross-user edit 404, HTMX swap shape).
- **V5 (MEDIUM)** — Alert delivery is the highest-risk surface: it crosses the ad publish transaction boundary (`transaction.on_commit`), spawns a daemon thread (`immediate_alerts.py:87`), and fans out to Telegram with a `Semaphore(10)` cap. Correctness depends on the ad-centric ↔ search-centric matchers (`alert_query.py:27,132`) being consistent. Partial coverage exists; the missing assertion is the `chat_id=None` skip + thread cap.
- **V6 (MEDIUM)** — The frequency/cadence contract is a spec-implementation mismatch: the top plan documents "daily/weekly cadence" and `last_notified_at` gating, but no frequency field exists and `last_notified_at` is never read. This is a correctness/expectation risk — either the spec must be corrected or the feature must be implemented. No test exists for the dedup-with-notifications dry-run path.

V5 and V6 are MEDIUM because an alert-delivery regression silently breaks a core buyer notification channel; the daemon thread and advisory-lock boundaries make it hard to detect without tests.
